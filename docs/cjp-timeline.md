# CJP & Abhijeet Dipke — Chronological Reconstruction (to 5 Oct 2026)

Oct 5, 2026 · @Johnson Varghese

## How to read this

The Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) went from an online joke on 16 May 2026 to forcing the Education Minister's resignation on 25 July, and is now (5 October) mobilising against Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar. This document reconstructs that arc day by day from published reporting (wire services, national and regional outlets, Wikipedia's tracked timelines), in English and Hindi.

Sourcing standard: every entry rests on at least one news report or encyclopaedia entry linked inline. Times of day are given only where a source states them. Where sources disagree on a date, both are noted.

**Two premises in the research brief are not supported by the record:**

- **"Abhijeet going back to the USA."** Dipke lived in Boston until early June and flew to Delhi on 5–6 June. No credible report shows him returning to the US afterwards. A viral claim that he flew back after Pradhan's resignation was fact-checked as false by The Quint's WebQoof: the video dated from 15 June, and no reliable report of a return exists ([source](https://www.thequint.com/hindi/webqoof/old-video-shared-as-abhijeet-dipke-returned-to-america-after-dharmendra-pradhan-resign)). A separate claim that he was "deported" in June was also false. In a simulation, model a single US-to-India return, not a round trip.
- **"Every post, hour by hour."** Individual X and Instagram posts are only discoverable here when news outlets quoted them. Many smaller local actions (city chapters, single school audits) are reported in aggregate, not one by one. Gaps are listed in the final section.

Names to know: Abhijeet Dipke (founder, national convenor); Saurav Das and Ashutosh Ranka (co-convenors, spokespersons); Sonam Wangchuk (most prominent ally).

## Before CJP: the exam crisis and the remark (to 15 May)

Two exam scandals set the stage: NEET-UG 2026 was cancelled over a paper leak, and CBSE's on-screen marking left thousands awaiting results ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Delhi_Jantar_Mantar_protests)).

### Early May — NEET-UG cancelled

- NEET-UG 2026 was conducted nationally, then cancelled by the National Testing Agency with government approval after leak allegations. The matter went to the CBI, which registered an FIR around 12 May ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Delhi_Jantar_Mantar_protests)).
- A re-test was later scheduled; the re-exam was held around 20 June.
- Organisers later said more than 20 students died by suicide amid the turmoil; families became central to the movement ([source](https://www.the-star.co.ke/news/world/2026-07-21-indias-cockroach-protest-continues-a-day-after-police-crackdown)).

### 15 May — The "cockroach" remark

- In the Supreme Court, a bench of CJI Surya Kant and Justice Joymalya Bagchi refused to hear a contempt plea against the Delhi High Court over delay in senior-advocate designation.
- While rebuking the petitioner's counsel, Kant compared unemployed youth who turn to media, social media and RTI activism to "cockroaches" and "parasites of society" ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cockroach_Janta_Party)).
- The context was fake law degrees; the bench said it awaited a case to order a CBI probe of lawyers practising on fake degrees.

### 16 May — The clarification

- Kant said media had misquoted him and he meant people entering "noble professions" on fake degrees; he said he held India's youth in high regard. The label stuck anyway ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cockroach_Janta_Party)).

### Who Dipke was on 15 May

- Born 29 September 1995 in Aurangabad (Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar), Maharashtra, into a Dalit family; journalism degree in Pune; MS in Public Relations, Boston University ([source](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/world/asia/abhijeet-dipke-profile-cockroach-janta-party-genz-neet-protests-india)).
- Worked with AAP's social media team 2020–2023, including meme content for the 2020 Delhi election; moved to the US in 2023. Said to be close to Manish Sisodia ([source](https://m.thewire.in/article/politics/abhijeet-dipke-reveals-how-cockroach-janta-party-was-born-details-aap-links)).
- He was job-hunting in the US when the remark broke ([source](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/lifestyle/abhijeet-dipke-started-cockroach-janata-party-cjp-enn26052101475)).

## May 16–31: launch, viral surge, crackdown

Within a week CJP passed BJP's Instagram following, had its X account withheld in India, and reported its website taken down ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cockroach_Janta_Party)).

### 16 May (Sat)

- Frustrated, Dipke posted the six words "What if all cockroaches come together?" It went viral ([source](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/world/asia/abhijeet-dipke-profile-cockroach-janta-party-genz-neet-protests-india)).
- He then posted a casual launch line, "Launching a new platform for all the 'cockroaches' out there," with a sign-up link ([source](https://www.thestatesman.com/india/what-is-cjp-cockroach-janata-party-abhijeet-dipke-manifesto-1503596075.html)).
- cockroachjantaparty.org went live: tagline "Voice of the Lazy & Unemployed"; mock eligibility (unemployed, lazy, chronically online, able to rant). Built with AI tools including Claude and ChatGPT ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cockroach_Janta_Party)).
- A five-point manifesto: no Rajya Sabha seats for retired CJIs; CEC arrest under UAPA if legitimate votes are deleted; 50% reservation for women; cancel Adani/Ambani media licences; 20-year ban for defectors.

### 17–18 May

- Two TMC MPs, Mahua Moitra and Kirti Azad, expressed support and were "welcomed" into CJP within two days; \~40,000 sign-ups reported by 18 May ([source](https://www.indiatoday.in/india/story/cockroach-janta-party-40000-members-mahua-moitra-kirti-azad-gen-z-convention-2913347-2026-05-18)).
- Activist Anjali Bhardwaj proposed two additions (CJP answerable under RTI; no secret "Cockroach CARES Fund"); the party adopted them.
- Hashtag #MainBhiCockroach spread.

### 19 May

- Akhilesh Yadav (SP) took a "BJP vs CJP" jibe ([source](https://www.hindustantimes.com/india-news/bjp-vs-cjp-akhilesh-takes-jibe-as-cockroach-janta-party-takes-internet-by-storm-101779288974991.html)).
- Instagram passed 3 million followers inside \~78 hours.

### 20 May

- Celebrities engaged: Anurag Kashyap, Kunal Kamra, Konkona Sen Sharma, Fatima Sana Shaikh, Dia Mirza, Esha Gupta. Dhruv Rathee said in a Short he would join ([source](https://www.mid-day.com/entertainment/bollywood-news/article/cockroach-janata-party-bollywood-celebrities-join-the-viral-satirical-political-outfit-23631405)).
- Lokmat reported his parents in Aurangabad were worried about his entry into politics ([source](https://www.lokmattimes.com/aurangabad/despite-his-viral-success-abhijeets-parents-worry-about-his-entry-into-politics/)).
- A student offshoot, the Cockroach Students' Union of India, was formed ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cockroach_Students%27_Union_of_India)).

### 21 May (Thu)

- Instagram passed 10 million, overtaking BJP's handle ([source](https://www.indiatoday.in/india/story/cockroach-janta-party-10-million-followers-on-instagram-bjp-followers-cji-remarks-genz-youth-2914805-2026-05-21)).
- MeitY ordered X to block the account under IT Act s.69A, citing IB inputs on national security; it was withheld in India at \~90,000 followers ([source](https://indianexpress.com/article/political-pulse/in-cockroach-janta-party-handle-government-sees-a-national-security-threat-asks-x-to-block-10702041/)).
- Minutes later CJP reappeared as "Cockroach is Back" (@Cockroachisback).
- AP, BBC, Guardian and Reuters ran international stories.

### 22 May

- Instagram at \~20 million; CJP announced it was time for "asli kaam" (real work) ([source](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/new-updates/asli-kaam-ka-waqt-aagya-cockroach-janta-party-launches-new-campaign-after-crossing-bjps-instagram-follower-count-hits-20-million/articleshow/131258883.cms)).
- Shashi Tharoor called it a "revelation" and the X block "disastrous" ([source](https://indianexpress.com/article/political-pulse/shashi-tharoor-cockroach-janata-party-reveals-people-frustrated-with-government-has-shown-opposition-there-is-an-opportunity-waiting-to-be-seized-10701728/)).
- TraceX Labs flagged a fake "CJP" Android APK spreading spyware via WhatsApp and Telegram.
- Prashant Bhushan urged using the platform to press Dharmendra Pradhan on NEET and a right-to-employment law.
- Local actions under the CJP banner: a Rohtak protest; a "CJP Karnataka" human-chain poster for Bengaluru Town Hall ([source](https://scroll.in/latest/1093036/cockroach-janata-party-founder-says-instagram-account-hacked-backup-handle-taken-down)).

### 23 May (Sat)

- 8:45 am: Dipke posted that the official Instagram, his personal account and backups were hacked; any later post was not official ([source](https://scroll.in/latest/1093036/cockroach-janata-party-founder-says-instagram-account-hacked-backup-handle-taken-down)).
- He said the government took down the website: "Why is the government so scared of cockroaches?" Hours later a new X account was opened.
- He clarified CJP had not called any protests; the petition for Pradhan's resignation had \~600,000 signatures and \~1 million sign-ups ([source](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/5/23/cockroach-janta-partys-founder-says-indian-government-took-website-down)).
- Sonam Wangchuk called himself an "honorary cockroach" ([source](https://www.newindianexpress.com/amp/story/india/2026/May/23/sonam-wangchuk-calls-himself-honorary-cockroach-backs-cjp-urges-centre-to-hear-youth)).
- BJP's Sukanta Majumdar claimed 49% of followers were Pakistani; Dipke posted demographics showing 94%+ Indian. Kiren Rijiju posted about followers from "Pakistan & Soros gang" without naming CJP.
- Dipke said he had received death threats; trademark applications for the name appeared.

### 24–26 May

- 24 May: Anna Hazare said the government should encourage the youth backing CJP ([source](https://indianexpress.com/article/india/anna-hazare-cockroach-janta-party-country-youth-encourage-10706506/)).
- 24–25 May: A Supreme Court plea sought a CBI probe of CJP's "activities"; CJI Kant told the lawyer not to take it "so sentimentally". It was dismissed ([source](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/dont-take-it-so-sentimentally-cji-tells-lawyer-who-complained-about-activities-of-cockroach-janta-party-fake-law-degrees/article71019997.ece)).
- 25 May: Derek O'Brien said Mamata and Abhishek Banerjee fully backed CJP ([source](https://www.telegraphindia.com/india/fighting-good-fight-mamata-abhishek-banerjee-express-full-support-for-cockroach-janta-party-derek-obrien-says/cid/2162301)).
- 26 May: Dipke moved the Delhi High Court against the X block ([source](https://m.economictimes.com/news/india/cockroach-janta-party-founder-abhijeet-dipke-moves-delhi-high-court-against-x-account-blocking/articleshow/131311593.cms)).

### 27–31 May

- A Karnataka BJP worker's PIL in the Allahabad HC (Lucknow bench) sought NIA/ED probes and Dipke's extradition; it was later dismissed (2 June).
- 30 May: Frontline and Al Jazeera's The Listening Post analysed the movement.

## June 1–19: going offline, the return, the city tour

Dipke flew from the US on 5 June, led the first Jantar Mantar protest on 6 June, then toured cities before an ultimatum date of 20 June ([source](https://thefederal.com/category/news/cjp-dipke-leaves-for-india-from-us-245523)).

### 1 June

- Dipke announced CJP would take its campaign offline with a protest ([source](https://www.britannica.com/topic/What-is-the-Cockroach-Janta-Party)).
- AISA and Krantikari Yuva Sangathan protested outside the Education Ministry over NEET, CBSE Class 12 and CUET lapses ([source](https://www.hindustantimes.com/india-news/we-are-not-scared-protests-outside-education-ministry-amid-neet-cbse-exam-controversies-101780315169677.html)).

### 2–4 June

- 2 June: Allahabad HC refused the BJP worker's PIL, telling him to approach the Karnataka HC ([source](https://www.livelaw.in/high-court/allahabad-high-court/allahabad-hc-bjp-worker-pil-probe-cockroach-janta-party-abhijit-dipke-536574)).
- \~3 June: Dipke told The Federal he was returning "for good" and was ready for arrest. Online claims said he was being deported; he replied he was "pretty much in the U.S." and would arrive 6 June ([source](https://x.com/abhijeet_dipke/status/2061840798115610873)).
- 3 June: CJP named three spokespersons: Saurav Das, Vijeta Dahiya, Ashutosh Ranka ([source](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/india/new-generation-of-leaders-cockroach-janta-party-appoints-three-spokespersons/articleshow/131479941.cms)).
- His mother later said she had urged him not to leave his US job.

### 5 June (Fri) — Departure

- Morning: Dipke posted "On my way to India… Leaving my fate in the hands of the Constitution. #JaiBhim" ([source](https://thefederal.com/category/news/cjp-dipke-leaves-for-india-from-us-245523)).
- He invited supporters to meet him at Delhi Airport; CJP said the response was "beyond our imagination".
- He said he would lead "with peace and love".

### 6 June (Sat) — Arrival and first protest

- Police deployed \~5,000 personnel in New Delhi district, including at IGI Airport ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Delhi_Jantar_Mantar_protests)).
- Dipke landed holding Ambedkar's autobiography as supporters and security swarmed him ([source](https://wdez.com/?p=970975)).
- Permission was granted until 5 pm as a "one-time exception". Organisers asked people to bring the national flag and a book.
- Sonam Wangchuk joined Dipke at Jantar Mantar. Hundreds came, some in cockroach masks.
- AIYF and AISF alleged RSS-linked people harassed and assaulted protesters ([source](https://thewire.in/rights/rss-will-not-deter-us-say-student-bodies-on-alleged-attacks-at-cjps-debut-jantar-mantar-protest)).
- Protest ended; The Hindu reported six detained. CJP set a seven-day deadline for Pradhan's resignation.

### 10 June

- CJP announced a Pune protest and an indefinite Delhi sit-in from 20 June if Pradhan stayed ([source](https://www.hindustantimes.com/india-news/cockroach-janata-party-cjp-protest-pune-indefinite-sit-in-in-delhi-june-20-dharmendra-pradhan-sacked-neet-cbse-101781093698326.html)).

### 11 June — Pune

- Nationwide campaign launched at Savitribai Phule Pune University, with Wangchuk present ([source](https://www.hindustantimes.com/india-news/cjp-protest-cockroach-janta-party-live-updates-today-pune-news-abhijeet-dipke-sonam-wangchuk-sspu-university-101781163877188.html)).
- Dipke released an Exam Manifesto with five demands, including ₹10,000 compensation for students hit by a leak, postponement or result delay ([source](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/nationwide-stir-over-exam-irregularities-cockroach-janta-party-to-release-education-manifesto/article71087965.ece)).
- Speech line: "Hindu vs Muslim politics won't work." Announced next stops: Jaipur, Lucknow, Amritsar, Bengaluru, then Jantar Mantar on 20 June.
- AP reported the launch of nationwide youth protests.

### 12 June — Lucknow

- CJP joined student protests over exam lapses; students said the government should talk about their issues, "not religion" ([source](https://indianexpress.com/article/cities/lucknow/students-protest-lucknow-cockroach-janta-party-joins-in-exam-jon-aspirants-10736322/)).

### 15 June — Bengaluru

- CJP demanded the minister's resignation at a Bengaluru protest ([source](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/state/cjp-demands-education-ministers-resignation-in-bengaluru-protest-over-national-exam-irregularities-enn26061500191)).
- Jaipur and Amritsar stops were announced; specific coverage of them was not found in this pass.

### 19 June (eve of the sit-in)

- Dipke posted photos of lorries at Jantar Mantar he said were loaded with stones, alleging authorities might let "goons" disrupt and blame CJP ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Delhi_Jantar_Mantar_protests)).

## June 20–30: the indefinite sit-in and Wangchuk's fast

The 20 June protest became an overnight occupation that lasted 36 days; Wangchuk began an indefinite hunger strike on 28 June ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Delhi_Jantar_Mantar_protests)).

### 20 June (Sat) — Second Jantar Mantar protest

- 1 pm: Protest begins. Hundreds, mostly students from across India, sing the national anthem and bang thalis with spoons (Dipke had asked people to bring them) ([source](https://www.outlookindia.com/national/cjp-stages-second-protest-at-jantar-mantar)).
- Same day: the NEET re-exam was held nationally.
- 5 pm: Permission expires; police declare the protest illegal. Dipke refuses to leave until Pradhan resigns ([source](https://www.msn.com/en-in/news/other/cjp-protest-in-delhi-live-protest-declared-illegal-as-5pm-deadline-ends-abhijeet-dipke-still-on-site/ar-AA266Ig9)).
- Evening: Dipke calls supporters, parents, teachers and students in Delhi to arrive by 6 pm.
- Police try to disperse the crowd, then withdraw without action.
- Night: CJP alleges police switched off lights and cut water, food and washroom access; later says water and lights were restored ([source](https://www.livemint.com/news/india/neet-ug-cjp-supporters-continue-overnight-protest-at-jantar-mantar-abhijeet-dipke-urges-students-parents-to-join-today-11781998613718.html)).
- Volunteer Mohammad Junaid, who supplied food and water and was thanked by Dipke, later told the Supreme Court police held him in a vehicle \~6 hours and dropped him in Dehradun.
- Wangchuk announced he would fast from 27 June if Pradhan did not resign.

### 21 June

- Overnight sit-in continues; Dipke urges students and parents to join with "Go Pradhan Go" ([source](https://www.thequint.com/news/politics/cockroach-janta-party-cjp)).

### 23 June — Pradhan hits back

- Pradhan called CJP a "B team of disruptive elements" (NDTV) and spoke of "tukde tukde" forces at Jantar Mantar (India Today); reports also quote "B-team of terrorists" ([source](https://www.ndtv.com/india-news/b-team-of-disruptive-elements-education-minister-dharmendra-pradhan-to-ndtv-on-cjp-11675087)).
- He said NEET may go fully computer-based next year and promised a crackdown on the "paper mafia".
- Dipke replied: "Sir, we are not terrorists. We do not need a certificate of patriotism from people like you."

### 25–26 June

- Day 6: Wangchuk posted a video giving the government until 27 June, else he would fast from 28 June. CJP reposted it as a "big announcement" ([source](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/sonam-wangchuk-to-begin-hunger-strike-in-delhi-on-june-28-if-dharmendra-pradhan-isnt-sacked-4052165)).
- 26 June: CJP launched a nationwide "Pradhan Go Back" campaign. Saurav Das inducted four new spokespersons: Vaishnavi Gaur, Aafreen Nawaz, Deepak Baliyan, Ratna Singh ([source](https://theprint.in/india/cjp-steps-up-protest-launches-nationwide-pradhan-go-back-campaign/2971012/)).

### 28 June (Sun) — Hunger strike begins

- Morning: Wangchuk posted that farm leaders from UP, Haryana and Punjab were under house arrest to stop them reaching Jantar Mantar ([source](https://newsarenaindia.com/nation/cjp-protest-wangchuk-begins-hunger-strike-at-jantar-mantar/81032)).
- Wangchuk and Dipke paid tribute at Rajghat.
- Afternoon: Wangchuk began his indefinite fast. Six AISA students, including national president Neha Bora, and independent student Hamad joined.
- CJP alleged police cut water and sanitation; police said they have no role in those facilities ([source](https://gdeltcloud.com/events/sonam-wangchuk-starts-hunger-strike-at-jantar-mantar-cjp-all--conflict_1502be51)).
- NCP (SP) spokesperson Anish Gawande joined and said TMC, DMK and NCP (SP) backed the agitation.

### 29–30 June

- 29 June: CPI(M) general secretary M.A. Baby and Brinda Karat visited and backed the protest ([source](https://thewire.in/rights/in-photos-sonam-wangchuk-left-students-hunger-strike-cjp-jantar-mantar)).
- Protesters arrived from Tamil Nadu, Jharkhand, Bihar, UP, Punjab, Haryana and Maharashtra. Krantikari Yuva Sangathan posters lined the site.

## July 1–19: the long sit-in to Wangchuk's removal

The camp held through 40°C heat while opposition support grew; on 18 July police removed the fasting Wangchuk and Dipke was detained, setting up the 20 July march ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Delhi_Jantar_Mantar_protests)).

### 1–9 July

- 1 July: BBC reported protesters still camped in over 40°C heat ([source](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cly0ey5y10qo)).
- 8 July (day 11 of the fast): Wire photo essay showed fasting students, Oggy-and-the-Cockroaches posters and independent creators livestreaming ([source](https://thewire.in/rights/in-photos-sonam-wangchuk-left-students-hunger-strike-cjp-jantar-mantar)).
- 9 July: Frontline reported the opposition lining up behind CJP ([source](https://frontline.thehindu.com/the-nation/neet-protest-opposition-support/article71201448.ece)).
- Volunteers and doctors ran food, water and medical stations throughout.

### 14–15 July

- 14 July: Wangchuk told The Indian Express the protest had "no political colour" and the PM should be sensitive ([source](https://indianexpress.com/article/political-pulse/sonam-wangchuk-interview-hunger-strike-cockroach-janta-party-protest-education-neet-10785080/)).
- Day 17: Several opposition leaders appealed to him to end the fast; organisers said he had lost 8.5 kg ([source](https://www.brut.media/in/articles/explained-why-the-jantar-mantar-protest-isn-t-over-and-why-sonam-wangchuk-is-still-fasting)).
- 15 July: A Delhi HC PIL challenged continuous police videography of protesters as an "atmosphere of intimidation"; it was admitted ([source](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/delhi/atmosphere-of-intimidation-pil-in-delhi-high-court-questions-continuous-surveillance-of-jantar-mantar-protesters/articleshow/132419094.cms)).

### 16–17 July

- 16 July: Arvind Kejriwal joined the sit-in and suggested Wangchuk as education minister. Owaisi urged the Centre to heed Wangchuk.
- BBC: 9.1 kg lost in 19 days; he could not stand unaided. A 1,800-signature appeal asked him to stop. A Delhi HC petition sought hospitalisation; the government said it would step in if health worsened ([source](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/crm0mg4z7edo)).
- 17 July (Fri): Health bulletin put his weight at 56.55 kg; doctors called it critical. He refused to stop without a government response ([source](https://www.theweek.in/news/india/2026/07/18/sonam-wangchuk-hunger-strike-jantar-mantar-delhi-police.html)).
- Mid-July: After a Delhi Police leadership change, barricades at the site were removed.
- Around this time a woman shouting Hindutva slogans threw ink at Dipke. The Express identified her as Barkha Trehan, a men's-rights activist ([source](https://indianexpress.com/article/delhi/abhijeet-dipke-ink-attack-barkha-trehan-jantar-mantar-cjp-protest-10792959/)).

### 18 July (Sat) — Removal of Wangchuk

- Dawn: Plainclothes police, posing as a medical team per one account, removed Wangchuk on day 21 of his fast ([source](https://www.dailypioneer.com/news/morning-drama-police-remove-wangchuk)).
- Police said it followed doctors' advice and Delhi HC directions; he was taken to Safdarjung Hospital.
- Dipke, who had briefly left, said he was beaten and detained. He alleged police "dragged Sonam Sir away while hurling abuses" ([source](https://www.theweek.in/news/india/2026/07/18/sonam-wangchuk-hunger-strike-jantar-mantar-delhi-police.html)).
- Police cleared the area; heavy security through the day.
- Dipke announced his own indefinite hunger strike and said the 20 July march would go ahead ([source](https://www.newindianexpress.com/india/2026/Jul/18/cjp-founder-abhijeet-dipke-starts-indefinite-hunger-strike-july-20-march-to-proceed-as-planned)).
- SFI announced its president Adarsh M. Saji and joint secretary Aishe Ghosh would join the fast. Left student groups joined the site.
- AAP MP Sanjay Singh called the removal an assault on democracy. Rahul Gandhi gave his first reaction, calling it wrong ([source](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/india/removal-of-sonam-wangchuk-wrong-rahul-gandhi-slams-bjps-asatya-and-hinsa-approach-in-first-reaction-to-jantar-mantar-protest/articleshow/132478450.cms)).
- Mumbai protests began running continuously from about this date.

### 19 July (Sun) — Eve of the march

- DCP Sachin Sharma posted on X that permission for the march was refused. Section 163 BNSS orders were imposed.
- Protests erupted in multiple cities; opposition condemned the removal ([source](https://www.financialexpress.com/india-news/support-grows-for-sonam-wangchuk-as-protests-erupt-in-multiple-cities-ahead-of-july-20-parliament-march/4295815/)).
- Wangchuk ruled out ending his fast and urged people to join the march. At his request, Dipke ended his own fast to lead the march.

## July 20 (Mon): the Sansad Chalo march

On the monsoon session's opening day, thousands marched toward Parliament; police used batons and tear gas, reporting 118 personnel hurt, while hospitals logged scores of injured protesters ([source](https://www.wunc.org/2026-07-21/indias-youth-led-cockroach-movement-vows-to-continue-protest-after-police-crackdown)). Exact clock times are scarce in reporting; the order below follows the sources.

**Morning — lockdown**

- CJP asked marchers to carry the flag, a copy of the Constitution or portraits of Ambedkar, Gandhi or Bhagat Singh, and no party flags ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Delhi_Jantar_Mantar_protests)).
- DMRC shut Udyog Bhawan, Central Secretariat, Patel Chowk, Janpath and Rajiv Chowk. Protests broke out inside stations as exits were sealed.
- Internet was cut near Parliament.
- From Safdarjung, Wangchuk issued a handwritten note: he would fast until youth leaders could meet MPs at Parliament or he could meet them in hospital.
- Three of the AISA hunger strikers ended their fast; Wangchuk continued in hospital.

**Late morning to afternoon — the march**
